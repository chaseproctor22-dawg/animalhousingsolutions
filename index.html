Sheet A-3Regulatory Notes

Built to the rules
that apply to you

Animal housing isn't just a structural question. Counties carry code requirements, permits carry specs, and an enclosure that doesn't meet them is a problem waiting to happen.

Every build is made to meet the county code requirements that apply where your enclosure is going — not a generic spec that happens to look right.

If your animal carries permit-specific caging requirements, bring the spec — dimensions, gauge, mesh, containment — and it gets built to that spec exactly.

If you're not certain what applies to what you're keeping, say so on the call. It's a better conversation to have before the steel is cut than after an inspection.

Sheet A-4Structural Notes

Twenty-nine years
hanging iron

This shop was started by a structural ironworker — the trade that stands up the steel skeletons of buildings. That's an unusual background for animal housing, and it's the entire difference.

An enclosure is not fencing. It's a structure. It carries load, takes weather, and gets climbed, chewed, leaned on and tested every single day for years by something with nothing else to do.

Built light, it works loose. You won't notice the day it does — you'll notice the day after.

There are copies of these designs in the field now. That's what happens when something works well enough to be worth stealing. What doesn't get copied is the weld, the material spec, and knowing which direction a joint is weak in before an animal goes looking for it.
